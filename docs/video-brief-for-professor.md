# Video brief for Listening Coach (for the Higgsfield videos)

Listening Coach trains Korean learners to understand **fast, natural English** by typing the missing words
in a short video clip. The video is not just something to watch: the learner replays one sentence many times,
slows it down, and types what they hear. So the **sound** matters more than the picture.

## 1. What each video should be
- **Length:** 30 to 60 seconds. **6 to 10 spoken lines.**
- **Each line:** 5 to 12 words, about 1.5 to 4 seconds long. Full, natural sentences.
- **Pause between lines:** about half a second of silence, so each sentence can be cut out and replayed.
- **Speakers:** one or two people. Never more than two. Nobody talks at the same time as someone else.
- **Setting:** an everyday place learners really meet: cafe, office, elevator, airport, hotel, shopping, a phone call, friends making plans, roommates.
- **Format:** `.mp4` (H.264), landscape 16:9, 720p is enough, **under 50 MB**. Start with about 1 second of silence. No end credits.

## 2. Audio rules (the most important part)
1. **Clean voice only.** No music, no background noise, no sound effects while people speak.
2. **No subtitles and no on-screen text** showing the words. The learner must listen, not read.
3. **Natural speed and rhythm.** Real conversation, not a slow "teacher voice" and not a robotic voice. Contractions and reductions are welcome: *gonna, wanna, gotta, kinda, lemme, didja, whatcha*.
4. **One accent per video**, clearly American (US) or British (UK). Tell us which one.
5. **The spoken words must match the script exactly.** AI voices sometimes change or skip a word. Please check, and tell us if a line was changed.
6. Original script only. **No lines from films or TV.** No real people's faces or voices.

## 2b. Please test first
Make a **10-second test** and listen to it carefully on headphones:
- Is the accent right (US or UK)?
- Does it sound like a real person, with natural speed and no robotic tone?
- Are the words exactly what the script says?
If yes, make the full video.

## 3. What helps learners most (our three target problems)
- **Speed (SP):** some lines spoken at normal fast speed, with small words like *a, the, to, of* said quickly.
- **New words (V):** each video has **1 to 3 useful expressions** said naturally, for example *on a whim*, *touch base*, *get the hang of it*, *I owe you one*. They should fit the situation and not feel forced.
- **Connected speech (CS):** words that blend together, for example *did you* sounds like *didja*, *want to* sounds like *wanna*, *pick it up* sounds like *pickitup*.

## 4. Three videos to start with
| # | Situation | Accent | Level | Speed | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Neighbours chat in an elevator | US | 2 to 3 | relaxed but natural | everyday small talk |
| B | Two colleagues plan a meeting | UK | 4 | normal | useful work expressions |
| C | Friends make plans for tonight | US | 5 to 6 | fast and casual | connected speech and reductions |

### Video A: elevator small talk (US, relaxed)
> A: Morning! Did you have a good weekend?
> B: Pretty good, actually. We went hiking up in the mountains.
> A: Oh, nice! Was the weather okay?
> B: It was a little cold, but we got lucky and it didn't rain.
> A: That sounds like a great trip. I just stayed home and relaxed.
> B: Honestly, that sounds pretty great too.
> A: Well, I hope you have a good day.
> B: You too! See you later.

### Video B: planning a meeting (UK, normal speed)
> A: Have you got a minute? I wanted to touch base about Friday.
> B: Of course. Is it about the client presentation?
> A: Yes. We're running a bit behind, so I need you to send me the slides by noon.
> B: I can do that. I'll just finish the last couple of pages first.
> A: Brilliant. And could you double-check the numbers?
> B: Sure thing. I'll let you know if I spot anything odd.
> A: Thanks. I owe you one.
> B: Don't mention it.

### Video C: plans for tonight (US, fast and casual)
Speak this the way friends really talk. Reduced forms such as "whatcha", "kinda", "gotta", "dunno" and "lemme" are what we want to hear.
> A: Hey, what are you doing tonight?
> B: Not much. I was kind of thinking about staying in.
> A: Come on! You've got to come out with us.
> B: Where are you guys going?
> A: I don't know yet. Maybe that new pizza place downtown.
> B: Alright, let me finish this and I'll meet you there.
> A: Awesome. Don't be late!

(The words above are the written script. The actors may say them in the relaxed, reduced way people really speak.)

## 5. A starting prompt for Higgsfield (edit as needed)
> A realistic, well-lit [place] scene. Two [adults] have a natural conversation, shown from a fixed camera,
> mostly medium shots with clear faces. Clean, close audio of their voices only, with no music, no background
> noise and no subtitles or on-screen text. [American / British] accent, natural conversational speed and
> rhythm, relaxed body language, short pauses of about half a second between lines. Total length about 45 seconds.
> Dialogue, spoken exactly as written: [paste the script].

- [ ] Every word matches the script (tell us about any change)
